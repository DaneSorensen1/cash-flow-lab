Check the reasoning, then choose support

Use component-level observations. These checks are instructional probes, not automated grades.

Pre-check key

Carrying amount $800 − $600 = $200. Gain $300 − $200 = $100. Proceeds $300 are the buyer’s actual payment.

Net Income$600
Subtract gain in Operating(100)

The gain increased Net Income, but the equipment-sale receipt belongs in Investing. Subtracting the gain removes the sale’s income effect from Operating. The Income Statement remains unchanged.

Carrying amount $1,400 − $1,000 = $400. Loss $400 − $250 = $150. No separate $150 cash payment occurred.

Timing check: income effect (500); cash effect (300); A/P adjustment +200. It reverses the unpaid expense’s income effect; it does not create a receipt.

Interpret the response

What you observeProbe and next move
Uses gain or book value as proceedsAsk what that number measures. Label all number roles and identify the buyer’s payment before teaching signs.
Proceeds right; gain added or loss subtractedPut the gain/loss into Net Income first. Ask which adjustment cancels its contribution to Operating.
Right amount, wrong sectionAsk for the intended section aloud; watch the control separately. Distinguish reconciliation from actual asset-sale cash.
All amounts right; cannot explain whyChange only selling price to book value and ask why Operating stays constant while Investing changes.
Loss is described as cash paidAsk who was paid the loss amount. Trace the sole receipt from the buyer.
Old M27 or a hint-use flagInspect the actual task if available. Do not infer laziness, guessing, misclicking, or a specific misconception from the flag alone.

Fast observation record

For each learner record: proceeds identified; number roles; Net Income effect; Operating line; Investing line; causal explanation; next probe. Use a fresh case after support, and recheck in the following class.

Ready for integration: correct gain and loss lines, an explanation of both jobs, and a correct sale-at-book-value counterfactual without hints.
