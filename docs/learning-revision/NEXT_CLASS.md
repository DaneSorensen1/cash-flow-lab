# Next class: rebuild the cash story

Suggested 45–50 minute session. Adjust from students' explanations, not from speed or a target completion percentage. Print STUDENT_CHECK.html; keep TEACHER_KEY.html for yourself.

## Before students arrive

Use the implementation instructions to deploy and smoke-test the patch. Keep existing progress and teacher access. Refresh both student and teacher tabs after deployment; do not clear site storage. Open a student's name for the new individual conversation plan. The curriculum browser also contains deeper teacher coaching even without records.

The provided source describes a class-wide pattern, not enough individual evidence to preassign named groups. Let the first check determine grouping. Old “wrong section” flags mix source and placement errors, and old gain/loss practice supplied answers.

## 0–5 minutes — identify the first missing link

Use the pre-check. Ask students to work quietly without the app, then put a question mark beside any answer they cannot explain. This is for instruction, not a grade.

Listen for:

- “I used $300 because it is what the buyer paid,” rather than “it was the biggest/smallest number.”
- “The gain increased Net Income, so removing that effect subtracts it in Operating.”
- “The full $300 proceeds belong in Investing because equipment is a long-term asset.”
- “The company did not pay out the loss amount.”

Have a learner say the intended section aloud before selecting the menu. Correct spoken reasoning plus a different click calls for a control demonstration; a wrong spoken explanation calls for reteaching.

## 5–17 minutes — one sale, two statement jobs

Use a company whose ordinary operations generate $500 profit and $500 cash. No other adjustments or investing/financing transactions. Equipment cost $800, accumulated depreciation $600, so carrying amount is $200. It is a cost allocation balance, not an appraisal.

| Row | Sale for $300 | Separate sale for $150 | Separate sale for $200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Profit from ordinary operations | $500 | $500 | $500 |
| Gain / (loss) | 100 | (50) | — |
| Net Income | **600** | **450** | **500** |
| Operating: remove gain / loss effect | (100) | 50 | — |
| Net operating cash | **500** | **500** | **500** |
| Investing: actual proceeds | 300 | 150 | 200 |
| Financing | — | — | — |
| Net increase in Cash | **800** | **650** | **700** |

Reveal one column at a time. Before revealing the loss column, ask what changes and what stays unchanged when only the buyer's payment changes. Before revealing the zero-gain column, ask whether Investing disappears. It does not.

Say: “The gain is a real measurement of income, but it is not an additional receipt. We remove the sale's income effect from the Operating reconciliation and report the actual receipt in Investing. We are not rewriting the Income Statement.”

Avoid “a loss means cash left,” “book value is what it is worth,” “profit is our best guess at cash,” or “add-backs create cash.” Do not use double-counting as the entire loss explanation; explicitly show (50) + 50 = 0 sale effect in Operating.

## 17–30 minutes — short groups based on the observed link

| Observed response | Teacher move | Release check |
|---|---|---|
| Gain/book value used as proceeds | Put cost, accumulated depreciation, carrying amount, gain/loss, and proceeds on separate labeled rows. Ask which number the buyer paid and what each other number measures. | Correctly identify proceeds and explain why the other numbers are not the receipt in a fresh case. |
| Proceeds correct, Operating sign wrong | Write the gain/loss into Net Income first. Ask for the adjustment that cancels its contribution to Operating. | Both gain and loss adjustments explained; ordinary operating cash unchanged. |
| Right amount and effect, wrong section | Ask whether the line reconciles Net Income or reports a long-term asset receipt. Compare spoken choice with the selected menu. | Independently place both lines and explain each job. |
| A/R increase called a cash receipt | Return to $1,000 work, $700 collected, $300 still owed; no expenses in this isolated transaction. | $1,000 income effect − $300 adjustment = $700 cash effect; explain a separate collection of an old receivable. |
| A/P increase called cash paid | Use $500 utility service used, $300 paid, $200 owed. Write negative income/cash effects. | (500) + 200 = (300); explain why the add-back is not a receipt. |
| All answers correct but explanation is only a rule | Change selling price to carrying amount, or ask why operating cash stays constant. | Causal explanation transfers to a new case without hints. |

Other learners use the revised gain/loss sequence or focused timing practice. Students who finish explain a zero-gain sale or an inventory purchase entirely on credit. Do not require them to repeat already understood problems for a completion counter.

## 30–40 minutes — one supported completion, one fresh problem

Students complete the app's worked-example gap and then a new-number check. Hide or close the worked example before the fresh check. If they use a hint, acknowledge the learning, then use a different case to check transfer.

Require the sentence: “This line uses ___ because that number measures ___. It belongs in ___ because ___.” For Operating, add: “It reverses the effect already in Net Income.”

## Final 5–10 minutes — exit check and next decision

Use the fresh loss case in STUDENT_CHECK.html. Collect a brief written explanation and the sale-at-book-value counterfactual. The key provides component-level interpretation.

For your notes, use these columns: learner; buyer's payment identified; carrying amount/gain/loss distinguished; effect on Net Income; Operating line; Investing line; explanation; next probe. Avoid a single aggregate correct/incorrect judgment.

- Needs number roles: cannot yet distinguish proceeds from carrying amount or gain/loss.
- Needs reconciliation: proceeds correct, but cannot explain reversing the income effect.
- Needs classification/navigation: accounting explanation is sound but source or destination selection remains inconsistent.
- Ready for integration: both lines and the counterfactual are correct with an explanation. Use a complete statement next, then check a new case in the following class.

These are temporary teaching groups, not fixed labels or automated grades.
